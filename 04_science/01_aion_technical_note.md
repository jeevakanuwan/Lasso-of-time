# AION Technical Note

A Faraday cage suppresses external electromagnetic fields by providing a conductive boundary in which induced charges redistribute to cancel the field inside.

AION borrows that boundary-condition philosophy but acts on a fictional chronometric field. Its superconducting toroidal lattice creates an unusually stable electromagnetic and quantum reference environment. Distributed atomic clocks measure proper-time differences; the control system minimizes phase drift.

The speculative leap is that the Veyr's temporal technology couples to this phase relationship.

AION therefore does not stop time. It keeps the local causal solution internally coherent while external history changes.

### Fictional axiom: observer reference isolation
The chamber's speculative boundary prevents an external temporal network from replacing the occupants' local causal reference with its imposed history. In the name **Acausal Isolation and Observer Nullification**, “observer nullification” means nullifying the external network's authority over the observer's reference state—not nullifying, erasing, or harming the observer. The name is an in-world technical label, while **the Stillhouse** is the human nickname.

AION preserves a causal reference, not a guaranteed return address. When coupled to the Veyr device, its reference can help a traveller retain continuity, but it does not guarantee arrival at the chamber's physical coordinates.

### The notebook equation

The equation that informs AION is a closed causal loop within the story. Adrian finds it before deriving it; after their reunion in the restored history, he and Mira use the Veyr device to transmit the same relation to the earlier notebook at the recorded moment. Its role is not to prove real-world time travel is possible. It is a fictional boundary condition that links AION's reference isolation to the Veyr network's inability to maintain mutually incompatible histories. The loop has no independent first author, and the characters knowingly complete it.

### Inside
Clocks tick, people age, experiments run and conversations occur.

### Outside
History can be overwritten, civilizations can disappear and centuries can pass.

### Limitations
Huge energy requirement, severe heat rejection, fixed installation, limited capacity and dangerous boundary crossing.
