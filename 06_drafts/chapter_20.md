# Chapter 20 — First Contact

Earth was blue, and nobody knew it was in danger.

Mira watched the planet turn beneath her as the temporal device completed its final spatial correction. The suit's display showed ocean, cloud cover, and a band of sunlight crossing the curve of the atmosphere. No orbital debris fields. No Veyr traffic. No military response. The planet's radio spectrum was crowded with human transmissions, most of them too weak to decode through the suit's emergency receiver.

The sound of it nearly undid her.

She had spent months living on a world where human language had vanished from every public system. Now the air around Earth carried broadcasts, navigation signals, weather reports, and ordinary conversations. People were speaking to one another without knowing that a woman from a future that no longer existed was listening above them.

She checked the destination again. The date fell before the Veyr invasion. The exact time remained uncertain, but the margin was enough for the mission. Earth had no knowledge of the Veyr. No government was preparing for first contact. No scientist had yet recorded the black fragments that would one day lead Adrian to AION.

There would be no warning. Mira could not send a message to Earth without risking the very timeline she was trying to save. Even if someone believed her, their response might alter the sequence in ways she could not predict. The mission was not to convince humanity to prepare for a war. It was to prevent the war from becoming necessary.

She activated the descent package. The temporal device had placed her above the planet, but its energy reserve was nearly depleted. The return route remained unresolved. She had one chance to move into the upper atmosphere, deploy the biological sample through the Veyr's operational environment, and leave before the device failed.

Except there was no Veyr operational environment on Earth.

The pathogen could not be sprayed into the atmosphere and expected to find its target. It needed a living Veyr carrier or biological cargo that would later travel through the FTL network. The Veyr had not arrived at Earth, but their civilization existed elsewhere in the same earlier history. Mira needed to reach a point where their ships, their personnel, or their biological logistics could be accessed.

She reviewed the device's stored transit coordinates. The temporal system had used a Veyr reference signature to resolve the jump. That signature included a remote network node, a stable anchor associated with the civilization's interstellar transport. The device had delivered her above Earth because that was the spatial coordinate she had selected, but the network signature remained available as a route back toward the Veyr system.

She could use it to locate an incoming ship—or attempt a second jump directly to the Veyr operational zone. The second option carried a high chance of stranding her if the device's remaining power could not sustain the return interlock.

Mira studied the sensor traces. A faint signal appeared at the edge of the receiver's range, not from Earth but from a point beyond the planet's orbital plane. It was weak, narrow, and consistent with the Veyr navigation signature. A vessel was moving through the local system, using a route that would bring it near Earth within the next few hours.

The Veyr had not invaded yet. Perhaps the vessel was surveying the system. Perhaps it had come for a different purpose. Whatever its mission, it carried the one thing LANTERN needed: Veyr biology already connected to the transport network.

Mira redirected the passive sensor and waited.

The vessel appeared as a dark shape against the stars, too small for the unaided eye and too smooth to resemble any human spacecraft. Its outer hull carried the same ribbed geometry as the material she had studied in the rewritten colony. It emitted no conventional radio traffic, but the phase detector registered a familiar pulse.

She had found her carrier.

Mira initiated the second-stage deployment sequence. The sample capsule was designed to transfer a controlled quantity of LANTERN into a sealed biological collection module, not to release the pathogen into open space. She would have to reach the vessel, access a suitable container, and leave the sample where it could be transported through the network. The plan depended on the Veyr treating her as an unknown object long enough for the transfer to occur.

She activated the temporal device.

The ring's remaining field opened around her in a narrow envelope. For a fraction of a second, the blue Earth vanished. The destination lock shifted from the planetary coordinates to the Veyr vessel's local reference. The device resolved the path, then dropped her into a small external service compartment attached to the ship's hull.

The compartment's lights came on.

Mira froze.

A sensor array had detected her arrival. The temporal device's field collapsed behind her, leaving the suit's emergency systems to maintain pressure. She had no time to return to Earth, and no energy to make another jump. The vessel's internal alarm began to pulse through the hull.

She attached the sample capsule to the service module's biological transfer port. The port recognised the container as an unclassified input. A mechanical seal opened. Mira transferred the prepared sample into the module's sterile reservoir and initiated the containment sequence. The transfer took four seconds.

The alarm changed pitch.

A light swept across the compartment. A seam opened in the far wall, revealing a corridor beyond it. Two Veyr figures entered wearing fitted protective layers. Their movements were controlled, not hurried. One carried a scanning instrument; the other held a restraint device.

Mira raised her hands and kept her body still.

The scanner passed over her suit, then stopped at the temporal device strapped to her back. The Veyr exchanged a sequence of clicks and pulses. The scanner flashed red.

They had found a human before any recorded human-Veyr contact. They had found a biological signature their databases could not classify. They had found a machine whose phase pattern resembled their own technology but did not match any registered model.

One of them stepped forward.

Mira could not understand its words, but she understood the posture: caution, curiosity, and the decision that she would not be allowed to leave.

The restraints closed around her wrists.

As they escorted her into the corridor, she looked once toward the service module. The sample reservoir was sealed. The transport system had accepted it. The virus had not yet spread, and she could not know whether the transfer had been enough. But the first link in the chain had been made.

The Veyr had captured her.

They had also carried their future vulnerability aboard their own ship.

Mira kept her face blank as the corridor doors closed behind her. Earth continued turning below, unaware of the war that might never come.
