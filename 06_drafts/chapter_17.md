# Chapter 17 — The Door

The Veyr temporal device had no door.

It was a ring of dark material suspended above a platform, with three nested arcs that rotated at different speeds. Fine markings ran along the inner surfaces. Some glowed when Adrian activated the control sequence; others remained dark, as if the machine were waiting for an input it had not yet received. The recovered device had been designed for a Veyr operator, and its controls assumed a biological reference pattern the humans could only approximate.

Mira stood on the platform, studying the ring.

“You're certain the destination window is before the invasion?”

“As certain as the model allows,” Adrian said. “The strongest stable anchor we can resolve lies several months before the first Veyr arrival in the original timeline. The local sky should be clear. No human detection system should have any reason to look for them.”

“Where will I emerge?”

“Above Earth. The device's spatial solution is less stable than its temporal one, but the model places you in high atmosphere, outside ordinary air traffic. You'll need to correct your position before entering the atmosphere.”

“Can the suit handle it?”

“For a short period. We have one descent package, and the guidance system is manual.”

Mira gave him a tired smile. “You do know how to make a person feel wanted.”

“I want you here.”

The words stopped both of them. Adrian had said them plainly, without a qualification or a model to hide behind.

Mira stepped down from the platform. “I want to be here too.”

He wanted to tell her not to go. He wanted to dismantle the device, seal the capsule, and find another route that did not require one of them to step through a machine they barely understood. But the Stillhouse had one viable external mission left, the anchor network was holding the rewritten history together, and the only plausible way to restore the original timeline was to introduce LANTERN before the Veyr operation began.

The virus could not be deployed into the present and expected to undo the past. It had to reach the Veyr civilization before their temporal attack on Earth. The pathogen would travel inside infected Veyr carriers, then spread through the FTL logistics network as crews and biological cargo moved between systems. If the synchronization dependency was as central as their evidence suggested, the outbreak could destabilize the distributed anchors before the rewritten history became self-sustaining.

Could. Not would.

Mira returned to the platform and checked the capsule seal. “We should be precise about the objective. I infect one carrier or biological shipment, verify the first transfer, and then leave the system. I don't stay to watch the outbreak.”

“Agreed.”

“If I can't reach a suitable carrier without exposing myself, I abort.”

“Agreed.”

“If the destination is wrong, I return immediately.”

Adrian hesitated. The Return Interlock could reject her reference state and route her to a different stable anchor. They had adjusted the controller, but there was no guarantee that the device would obey their modification.

“If the destination is wrong,” he said, “you use the interlock. Don't try to correct a bad jump from inside the field.”

“And if the interlock sends me somewhere else?”

“You find the nearest stable reference and try again.”

“That could be years away.”

“I know.”

She studied him. “You're trying to turn goodbye into a procedure.”

“It's the only way I know how to make it useful.”

“Then make the procedure useful. Don't make it a substitute for saying what you mean.”

He looked down at their joined hands. “I love you. I don't want you to go. I also know that if we do nothing, the Veyr keep a history in which neither of us ever existed. I won't ask you to stay just so I can be less afraid.”

Mira closed her eyes for a moment. When she opened them, they were wet.

“I love you too,” she said. “And I'm afraid. But I can live with fear better than I can live with knowing we had one chance and refused to take it.”

They spent the next hours checking the destination sequence. Adrian loaded the Stillhouse's local reference into the interlock and added a physical override that would force the device to attempt a return to the chamber before resolving any secondary anchor. He could not guarantee the override would be accepted, but it was a better safeguard than the original configuration.

Mira memorized the emergency sequence. She carried the LANTERN capsule in a shielded container, along with a compact biological sampler, a manual timer, and a paper card listing the deployment criteria. No networked device would accompany her. If the rewritten history altered electronic records, the paper card would remain a record of what she had intended to do.

At the final test, the ring brightened and a thin distortion appeared in the air above the platform. The sensors recorded a local phase discontinuity. For a fraction of a second, the chamber's external camera showed a sky filled with stars that did not match any position in the current celestial map.

Then the distortion closed.

Mira watched the trace. “That was the destination?”

“A preview of the boundary condition. Not the destination itself.”

“Can I see it again?”

“Not without stressing the ring.”

She nodded. “Then we don't.”

The room seemed smaller after the test. Every object had become part of the departure: the table where they had planned the virus, the clocks that had kept their memories intact, the seed packet waiting by the hatch, the photograph in the drawer. Adrian wanted to memorize every detail. He knew that memory could not protect him from loss, but he did it anyway.

Mira put on the transit suit. She checked each seal herself, then let Adrian check the back of the neck ring. Their hands brushed at the clasp.

“If I don't return,” she said, “don't spend your life inside this room.”

He could not answer immediately.

“Adrian.”

“I'll search for you if there's a way. If there isn't, I'll make sure the record survives.”

“And if you find me in a history where I don't know you?”

“Then I'll introduce myself.”

She laughed through the tears. “You'd be terrible at it.”

“I have a very good research record.”

“You have a terrible opening line.”

He pressed his forehead to hers. For a moment, neither moved.

Then Mira stepped onto the platform.

The ring began to rotate. The control surface recognized the biological reference pattern and opened a narrow window through the local causal boundary. The chamber clocks registered the field's formation. The Stillhouse's passive record started a new line.

Adrian stood at the console, one hand on the manual override.

“Destination confirmed,” he said.

Mira looked at him through the distortion. “I'll see you on the other side.”

The field brightened until he could no longer see her face.

Then the platform was empty.

The clocks continued to run. The chamber remained stable. The Return Interlock displayed a single status line:

**TRAVELLER IN TRANSIT — CAUSAL ANCHOR UNRESOLVED**

Adrian did not move until the line disappeared.
