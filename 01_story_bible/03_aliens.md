# The Veyr

## Civilization

The Veyr are a distributed interstellar civilization whose power depends on three connected systems:

- biological compatibility across their species,
- faster-than-light / warp transportation,
- temporal engineering.

These systems make their empire powerful, but also create a single point of failure.

## Biology

The Veyr possess a conserved molecular pathway that is absent in humans. Mira's team develops a fictional, species-specific viral agent that exploits this dependency.

The virus should not be a generic plague. Its behavior must be consistent with the novel's fictional biology, and its spread should be dramatized through the Veyr's own logistics rather than explained as a magical universal contagion.

## FTL network as transmission pathway

Veyr ships regularly move living crew, medical organisms, food cultures and biological materials between planets. Their warp / FTL network makes interstellar travel routine enough for infection to move between distant systems before symptoms are recognized.

The virus is deployed before the Veyr invasion of Earth. Earth has no knowledge of the aliens and does not become the source of the outbreak. The Veyr unknowingly carry the infection home and spread it through their own network.

## Temporal strategy

The Veyr decide that the safest victory is to erase humanity's causal development before the species can challenge them.

They believe history can be edited without meaningful consequences.

## Fatal mistake

The Veyr treat temporal causality as controllable and their FTL logistics as safe. Their attempt to erase humanity instead creates the conditions under which humanity's last weapon spreads through the Veyr network.

## Fate after the collapse

The Veyr are not universally erased when their imposed history collapses. The baseline human civilization returns, and the Veyr civilization continues in the restored history, but LANTERN causes heavy casualties across its biological coordination systems and temporal infrastructure. Some Veyr survive. The victory is the end of their anti-human temporal operation, not the extermination of an entire species. This distinction preserves the moral weight of the protagonists' choice and avoids making timeline restoration a magical universal kill switch.
